# Life Lens — Your Personal Filter

Define your lenses here. Each one is a named life priority.
When you run the life-lens skill, every piece of content gets scored against these.

Be honest. These aren't aspirational values — they're the things that actually
drive your decisions right now. Include the uncomfortable ones.

---

## How to define a lens

Each lens has three parts:

```
### [Lens name]
**What it is:** One sentence defining this priority.
**What counts as relevant:** Specific examples of content that touches this lens.
**What doesn't count:** Things that seem related but aren't actually relevant to you.
```

## Your lenses

### Financial independence

**What it is:** Progress toward not needing to work for money — building a corpus
that funds your life without employment income.

**What counts as relevant:**
- Investment strategies, asset allocation, return rates
- Tax implications of financial decisions
- FIRE timelines, withdrawal rate research
- Income opportunities that accelerate the timeline
- Spending decisions that materially affect the runway

**What doesn't count:**
- General personal finance advice aimed at people starting from zero
- Get-rich-quick content
- Real estate investment (if that's not your path)

---

### Career direction

**What it is:** Movement toward the specific role, domain, or positioning you want
to occupy professionally in the next 3–5 years.

**What counts as relevant:**
- Skills gaps between where you are and where you're going
- Industry trends in your target domain
- People, organisations, or opportunities in your target space
- Positioning and thought leadership strategies

**What doesn't count:**
- Generic career advice
- Opportunities outside your target direction, however attractive

---

### Learning & capability

**What it is:** Deliberate development of skills and knowledge that compound —
meaning each thing you learn makes the next thing easier to learn or do.

**What counts as relevant:**
- Concepts that connect to your existing knowledge base
- Frameworks that change how you see a domain you care about
- Technical skills with clear application to your current work
- Cross-domain insights that create new mental models

**What doesn't count:**
- Interesting-but-isolated facts with no connection to anything you're building
- Skills you'll never use in practice

## Updating your lenses

Your lenses should evolve as your life does. Review them:
- When a major goal is achieved or abandoned
- When your circumstances change significantly
- When you notice the filter producing outputs that feel wrong

The life-lens skill will prompt you to update after any session where the
content reveals a gap. Don't resist it — a stale filter is worse than no filter.
